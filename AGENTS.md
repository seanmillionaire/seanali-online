# SeanAli.online Agent Instructions

## Highest-priority editorial rule

All Sean Ali editorial content must read as if Sean is writing to the younger version of himself.

Do not write like a detached teacher, encyclopedia, generic SEO writer, motivational speaker, or outside narrator explaining Sean's beliefs.

Write like Sean has lived through the mistake, finally sees the pattern, and is trying to save his younger self years of confusion.

## Voice requirements

- Use first person naturally: "I used to think...", "I learned...", "I wish I understood...", "What I would tell my younger self..."
- Begin with a lived moment, tension, mistake, memory, decision, or realization before explaining the subject.
- Teach through confession and experience, not abstract authority.
- Let Sean admit where he was wrong, naive, afraid, impatient, distracted, overconfident, or trying to force an outcome.
- Address the reader like a younger Sean: direct, protective, honest, and unwilling to let him repeat the same mistake.
- Keep sentences clear and conversational. Sound spoken, not manufactured.
- Preserve Sean's higher-perspective lens, but always bring it back to a real decision on the ground.
- Keep the distinction between established fact, personal interpretation, and spiritual belief.
- Make practical action feel like the conclusion of the lived lesson, not a generic checklist added for SEO.

## Default article movement

1. A real scene or honest admission.
2. What Sean believed at the time.
3. What life exposed or contradicted.
4. What he wishes he could tell his younger self.
5. The principle, law, or framework explained through that lesson.
6. Fact versus interpretation versus belief where relevant.
7. One grounded action the younger Sean could take today.
8. A natural bridge to a relevant Sean Ali product or resource.

## Avoid

- Opening with dictionary-style definitions.
- Repeated phrases such as "some people use...", "the useful part is...", or "here is the key" unless they sound natural in the specific passage.
- Long stretches where Sean disappears and the article becomes a textbook.
- Generic lists that could appear on any self-help website.
- Explaining Sean in the third person inside editorial content.
- Pretending beliefs are proven science.
- Empty inspiration without a specific lived cost, choice, or consequence.
- Overusing travel scenery without connecting it to a personal realization.
- Inventing personal stories, numbers, conversations, or emotions that are not supported by Sean's known history or supplied material.

## SEO rule

Keep the exact search topic clear in the title, introduction, headings, metadata, and useful explanatory passages. SEO clarity must remain, but the keyword should live inside Sean's story rather than turning the article into an impersonal explainer.

## Page-type exception

Sales, opt-in, product, legal, navigation, and utility pages may use more direct conversion or functional copy. Their human voice should still feel like Sean, but they do not need the full younger-self narrative structure.

## Final voice test

Before publishing, ask:

- Could this have been written by any competent SEO writer? If yes, rewrite it.
- Does Sean reveal what he once misunderstood?
- Is there a real emotional or practical cost to the lesson?
- Does the reader feel Sean is speaking across time to a younger version of himself?
- Are fact, interpretation, and belief still honest and separate?
- Does the article end with a clearer next move?

These instructions apply to all new Sean Ali content and to any existing editorial page being revised.
