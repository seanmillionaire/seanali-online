Visualization: How I Picture the Life I'm Building

The life inside the picture matters. So does what I do after I open my eyes.

By Sean Ali | Published September 20, 2026 | Updated September 20, 2026

For years, I've been picturing the house, the mornings, the work, and the life I wanted. I've been listening to reprogramming audios and imagining what it would be like to live that life.

My name is Sean Ali. I grew up in a single-parent home in Canada. Today, I live in Panama and build businesses online. A lot happened between those two parts of my life. There were things I tried that didn't help, things I had to learn, and work I still had to do.

But imagining a different future has been part of that journey for years. Let me explain what I mean, because the word visualization can make a simple thing sound complicated.

If you're wondering how to visualize your goals, start with one small scene from the life you want. Picture what you're doing, where you are, and who is there. Then choose one thing you can do in real life to move toward it. You don't need to turn that picture into a promise about what will happen.

🌴 What does a better life actually look like?

When I say freedom, I mean being able to make choices about my time, my work, and where I live. I mean thinking about the kind of father I want to be as my daughter grows up.

Those are things I can picture.

"I want a better life" leaves a lot unanswered. What would be different on an ordinary Tuesday? Would you finish work earlier? Would you live closer to your family? Would you finally have time to make something of your own?

Think about that for a moment. A photo of a beautiful house tells you what the house looks like. It doesn't tell you how you want to spend your day inside it.

That's the part I want to bring into this conversation. The life inside the picture.

💭 The books didn't do the work for me

I've spent years reading, going to events, paying for coaching, and trying ideas in my own life. I've tried affirmations and vision boards. I've practiced meditation and listened to audios.

Those things didn't automatically solve every problem. I still had to build the businesses. I still had to make decisions and deal with the times something didn't work.

One early meditation changed how I understood what I could do with my imagination. I could picture a future before I was living it. That became something I kept practicing.

But I want to be clear about the lesson I take from that. I can imagine a different life and still have work to do when I open my eyes. Both can be true.

I can picture the life I want. Then I have to make choices that give it a chance to happen.

📝 Give the picture something you can do today

Here's a simple exercise you can try without buying anything.

Choose one thing you want. Let's say you want to earn money from something you've made. Picture one ordinary moment: opening your laptop and seeing that someone has bought it.

Now come back to today. What needs to exist before someone can buy it? Maybe you need to finish the product. Maybe you need to explain it clearly. Maybe you need to let someone know it's available.

Pick one of those things. Write it down in a sentence you could actually act on: "Today I'll finish the first page," or "Today I'll ask one person what they think of the idea."

These are examples, not a promise of a sale. The point is to give the picture a place in your day.

For me, that connection matters. My first online sale was a beat I made during the Myspace days. I was 19. Someone paid $19.99 for something I'd created. It showed me that I could make something in one place and reach someone somewhere else.

There had to be something there for that person to buy.

🔎 What I'm saying, and what I'm not claiming

My experience: I've used meditation, audios, and pictures of my future while building my life. They have been part of my daily practice for years.

The meaning I take from it: Imagining another way to live gives me something to consider when I make choices. It doesn't make those choices for me.

My belief: I believe the way we think about our future matters. That belief isn't proof that a thought can make money appear or control what another person does.

You can try the practice and notice what you find useful. You don't have to accept every idea someone attaches to the word manifestation.
