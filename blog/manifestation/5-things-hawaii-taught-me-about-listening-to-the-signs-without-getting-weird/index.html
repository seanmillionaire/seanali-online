5 Things Hawaii Taught Me About Listening to the Signs Without Getting Weird

I still notice signs. I just do not let them make my decisions for me. Hawaii helped me understand the difference.

Read The 5 Lessons
Written by Sean Ali · Updated August 25, 2026

Look at the photo above.

I am standing on a beach in Hawaii.

Years earlier, a life where I could work online and travel felt very far away. I grew up in a single-parent home in Canada. I faced cancer at 15. I did not begin with money, connections, or some secret advantage.

I found online business. I studied meditation and visualization. Most importantly, I kept making choices that moved my life in a different direction.

Years later, I was in Hawaii living a moment I had once only imagined.

It would be easy to call that a sign from the universe.

Maybe it was. That is a matter of faith.

But I know this much: the sign did not book the flight, build the business, or do the work.

A sign may get your attention. Your next choice is what gives the moment value.

Why Signs Can Feel So Personal

You see 11:11 after asking for direction. A song plays when you are thinking about somebody. A sentence in a book answers the question you carried all week.

The moment feels personal because it connects something outside you with something already alive inside you.

That does not make you foolish. It makes you human.

The mistake begins when you stop thinking, hand the moment all your power, and wait for another symbol to tell you what to do.

The 5 Hawaii Lessons

1

Notice the sign without surrendering your judgment.

You are allowed to pause when something feels meaningful. You are also allowed to ask whether it is true, useful, and connected to the life in front of you.

2

The meaning you choose changes what you notice next.

If 11:11 reminds you to be brave, you may notice the call you have been avoiding. The number did not make the choice. It helped you see a choice that was already there.

3

Peace gives you room to see clearly.

Hawaii gave me distance from normal routines. That distance helped me see what mattered. Peace was not the answer by itself. It gave me enough room to hear my own answer.

4

Real guidance does not ask you to abandon responsibility.

If your interpretation tells you to ignore facts, risk money you cannot lose, or wait forever, slow down. Wisdom can live beside faith. You do not have to choose one or the other.

5

The best response is one honest action.

Write the page. Make the call. Have the conversation. Apply for the job. Rest when you need to rest. A meaningful moment should bring you back into your life.

Fact, Interpretation, and Faith

The fact: Human brains are built to detect patterns. Researchers have shown that we notice regularities in our surroundings even when we are not deliberately looking for them.

My interpretation: Seeing a repeated number or hearing a perfectly timed song is a real experience. It is not automatic proof that an outside force is giving you an order.

My faith: I believe God can use ordinary moments to make us stop, reflect, and remember what matters. I also believe we are responsible for testing what we think a moment means.

The Four Questions I Use Now

When something feels like a sign, I do not dismiss it. I ask four simple questions.

1

What actually happened?

State the event without decorating it. I saw 11:11. I heard a song. A person called.

2

What meaning did I give it?

Be honest about the story you added. That story may be helpful, fearful, hopeful, or completely wrong.

3

Does that meaning make me wiser?

Good meaning brings clarity, courage, care, and responsibility. Bad meaning makes you passive, reckless, or dependent on the next sign.

4

What can I responsibly do today?

Choose one action that still makes sense even if the moment was only a coincidence.

What Hawaii Really Taught Me

Hawaii did not teach me to chase symbols.

It taught me to pay attention.

Pay attention to beauty. Pay attention to what brings peace. Pay attention to the life you keep saying you want. Then pay even closer attention to the choices you make when nobody is watching.

I still enjoy those strange little moments when life seems to answer back.

I just remember that a sign is an invitation to become more awake, not an excuse to become less responsible.
