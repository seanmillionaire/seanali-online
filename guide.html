Sean AliMoney, the mind, and what I practice

The money story in your head becomes the one you keep rehearsing.

Here's the simple before-bed practice I use to picture a different financial life — without pretending meditation replaces action.

9 short chapters · about 4 minutes
01

You can want more money and still rehearse the opposite

You can want a better income.

More savings. More freedom.

And still spend most of the day thinking about what could go wrong. Checking the account. Thinking about what you cannot afford. Telling yourself you're behind. Looking at opportunities through the lens of what you do not have.

I've done this too.

After years of studying the mind while building businesses, one thing keeps standing out to me:

What I keep noticingWhat you repeatedly think about affects what you notice, what you expect, and what you do next.

Not magic. Not destiny. Just something worth paying attention to.

Sean's takeBelief without action does not build the life. But action gets a lot harder when your mind keeps rehearsing the opposite of where you want to go.
02

Money has been personal for me since I was a kid

I grew up in a single-parent home in Canada.

Money pressure was real. I know what an empty fridge feels like.

Years later, I learned how to build businesses and make money online. But making money did not automatically erase everything I had learned to think about money.

That is part of why I became so interested in the mind.

For 15 years and counting, I've read, studied, paid for courses and coaching, practiced meditation and visualization, built businesses, made mistakes, changed my mind, and kept testing what actually helps me.

This guide is one small piece of what has held up for me.

03

Your mind is practicing something every day

Think about this.

When you replay a bad conversation in your head, you are practicing it again.

When you picture everything going wrong tomorrow, you are practicing that too.

When you clearly picture something you want and then take a useful action toward it, that is also practice.

I do not use visualization as a substitute for reality. I use it to decide what picture I want my mind returning to.

  • What do I actually want?
  • What would that life look like on an ordinary Tuesday?
  • What would I do differently if I believed it was possible?
  • What can I do today that moves me one step toward it?
Keep both partsThe picture matters. Then the action matters.
04

What are you rehearsing about money?

You do not need to diagnose yourself.

Just listen to the sentences already running through your head.

“There's never enough.”
“I'll deal with it later.”
“People like me don't make that kind of money.”
“If I make more, I'll probably lose it.”
“I need to learn one more thing before I'm ready.”
“I already know what I should do. I'm just avoiding it.”

You do not need a label for any of this. Notice the sentence. Ask whether it still serves you. Then decide what you want to practice instead.

05

Why I like doing this before bed

I like the few minutes before bed because the day is finally quiet.

No meetings. No notifications. Nobody asking me for anything.

It gives me a clean moment to stop feeding my mind random input and deliberately picture what I'm building.

Sometimes I visualize. Sometimes I listen to one of my reprogramming audios. Sometimes I just ask myself a better question.

I'm not telling you seven minutes guarantees anything. It doesn't.

The pointIf you are going to spend those last few minutes thinking anyway, you can choose what you rehearse.
06

Try this tonight

  1. 1Put the phone down. Give yourself a few quiet minutes.
  2. 2Choose one financial outcome you actually want. Not ten things. One.
  3. 3Picture one ordinary scene that would be true if it happened. Paying something without stress. Seeing a stronger month in your business. Looking at savings you deliberately built. Having the freedom to book the trip.
  4. 4Notice what your mind immediately says back. Do not fight it. Just notice it.
  5. 5Decide one action you will take tomorrow. Send the message. Publish the offer. Review the numbers. Ask the question. Learn the skill. Make the call.
  6. 6Then relax. If you want, this is also where I use a guided money meditation.
RememberThe point is not to pretend. The point is to stop letting the worst-case picture be the only picture your mind sees.

Use the audio. Then do something.

This part matters.

Do not turn meditation into another way to avoid your life.

If you listen, pair it with movement.

  • Send the proposal.
  • Publish the product.
  • Ask for the raise.
  • Look at the numbers.
  • Apply for the opportunity.
  • Save the money.
  • Learn the skill.
  • Make the call.

Your action will be different from mine. That is fine.

The important part is that the mental practice eventually points back toward reality.

Sean's takeI use the mind work to support action — not replace it.
09

Choose what you rehearse tonight

You are going to think about something before you fall asleep.

You can replay the same worries.

Or you can spend a few minutes picturing the life you are trying to build.

Then wake up tomorrow and do something that moves you toward it.

That is the whole idea.
