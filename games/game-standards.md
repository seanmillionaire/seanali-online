# Sean Ali Games Standard

Use this for every new game and every game update.

## First screen
- The first screen must clearly show what to read first.
- Add movement, glow, arrows, or pulsing focus on the first thing the child should read.
- Use simple numbered steps when the game starts.
- Keep the first instruction ultra clear: what do I do right now?
- Use icons and emojis to help a young mind see the meaning.

## Copy
- No long in-game sentences in titles, choices, or answers.
- Break long thoughts into short lines.
- Use simple 7-year-old words.
- Use image-making words and emojis.
- A choice should be quick to read.

## Game depth
- Level 1 is the warm-up.
- Level 2 should require more thinking.
- The child should not finish everything in one hour.
- The game should reward coming back across many sessions.

## Sound
- Add a small intro jingle on the first screen.
- Correct voice feedback: “Correct, you got it!”
- Wrong voice feedback: “Incorrect.”
- Do not let ElevenLabs read long wrong-answer explanations after the choice.
- Written hints are fine, but voice feedback stays short.

## Design
- Guide the eye.
- Highlight the current focus.
- Use movement with purpose.
- Do not make the screen visually noisy.
- The child should always know where to look and what to tap next.
